### Implementation notes

- **eb_jepa** is the backbone we chose over frozen V-JEPA 2 (see §5). Lightweight (~15 M params), action-conditioned, trains on our game data, fits 8 GB VRAM.
- **LeWorldModel** (~15 M params, single GPU, *two* loss terms only: next-embedding prediction + Gaussian regularizer) is the closest published architecture to ours. It introduced the spirit of the `ratio = val_pred / val_copy` gate we use (ratio < 1 beats copy-last; sweet spot ~0.93 for Treechop).
- **VICReg** prevents JEPA collapse (all embeddings → constant). Our `batch_var` monitor is the run-time check: < 1e-6 = collapse in progress.

### Foundational theory

| Paper | Authors | ID / link | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures Focus on Slow Features | Sobal et al. | [arXiv:2211.10831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10831) (2022) | JEPAs over-attend to slow, task-irrelevant features (textures, lighting) — exactly the trap on Minecraft's near-static background. Grounds our masking + VICReg choices. |
| LeJEPA: Provable and Scalable Self-Supervised Learning Without the Heuristics | Balestriero & LeCun | [arXiv:2511.08544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.08544) (2025) | Isotropic Gaussian = optimal embedding distribution; SIGReg objective. Collapse-free without stop-grad/EMA — a possible future simplification of our anti-collapse stack. |
| How JEPA Avoids Noisy Features: The Implicit Bias of Deep Linear Self-Distillation Networks | Littwin, Saremi, Advani, Thilak, Nakkiran, Huang, Susskind (Apple) | [arXiv:2407.03475](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03475) (2024) | Proves JEPA is implicitly biased toward *high-influence* (predictive) features, unlike MAE which chases high-variance (often noisy) ones. The theory behind why we don't reconstruct pixels on Minecraft's noisy POV. |
| Why and How Auxiliary Tasks Improve JEPA Representations | Yu et al. | [arXiv:2509.12249](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12249) (NeurIPS 2025) | **Directly justifies our WM v4 design.** Proves a *No Unhealthy Representation Collapse* theorem: a JEPA with an auxiliary regression head trained jointly with latent dynamics keeps non-equivalent observations distinct. Our `InventoryDynamics` head (inventory-as-state) is exactly this auxiliary-anchor pattern. |
| On the Identifiability of Controlled World Models | Zhang, Guan, Zhang, Zhang, Li | [arXiv:2607.22430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22430) (2026) | *(New, 2026-07-24.)* Proves conditions — spectral separation, plus sufficient action-space variation — under which an action-conditioned JEPA with Gaussian latents provably recovers the true state and dynamics (up to orthogonal transform). Directly frames our eb_jepa/WM v4 setup: the standing brightness/domain confound (attempts #7-#17, `docs/10`) may partly reflect *insufficient action-coverage overlap* between Treechop's training distribution and Obtain's spawn distribution, not only a photometric shortcut — an identifiability failure, not just a visual one. |
| The SIGReg Objective as Variational Free Energy: A Theoretical Active-Inference Account of JEPA World Models | Arnez, Gomez-Villa | [arXiv:2607.13612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13612) (2026) | *(New, 2026-07-15.)* Formally ranks anti-collapse regularizers on an entropy-estimator hierarchy: **VICReg and LogDet are "unsafe" upper bounds, PairDist is a safe lower bound, and SIGReg (LeJEPA, see above) eliminates the miscalibration gap entirely**, yielding an exact information bottleneck. A direct theoretical critique of our own collapse-prevention choice (`mine_jepa/ebwm/losses.py` uses VICReg) — read before any future anti-collapse rework. |
| Depth-Regularized JEPA World Models Learn More Transferable Representations from Real Outdoor Robot Data | Khan | [arXiv:2607.16314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16314) (2026) | *(New, 2026-07-15.)* An 18M-param JEPA world model + SIGReg on real outdoor robot video, with a depth-supervision auxiliary term added: -33% visual-odometry probe error, and better in-domain **and out-of-domain** (TartanGround benchmark) surprise-score separation under domain shift, at no inference-time cost. **The clearest published instance yet of attempt #15's own conclusion** (`docs/10`) — that our brightness/scene-composition confound, structural to `ebwm.pt`'s frozen photometric-only representation (attempts #7/#11/#14/#15/#17), needs "additional structure... a different modality entirely." Here depth is exactly that modality, and it demonstrably fixes an analogous domain-shift problem in a JEPA world model. Motivated attempt #18 Diagnostic 1 — whose Gate B (brightness-independence, r=0.045) passed and replicated, but whose Gate A (tree-close vs open separation) did **not** hold at a larger hand-labeled sample. |
| Asleep at the Wheel: JEPA's Limitations in Evaluating Novel Driving Data | Pavuluri, Karkhanis, Mushtaque | [arXiv:2608.01336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01336) (2026) | *(New, 2026-08-02.)* **The closest published analogue to this project's own central failure.** Scores driving clips by the prediction-error "novelty" of a frozen V-JEPA + lightweight predictor head. Under a cross-dataset protocol the method looks highly effective — then **collapses to chance on a fair single-dataset benchmark**, on par with no-training baselines: "cross-dataset protocols can silently reward domain separation over novelty." A lightly supervised probe on the *same frozen embeddings* nearly doubles average precision, so **"the bottleneck is indeed the self-supervised objective, rather than the representation."** This is attempt #17 Prong A restated by an independent group: our Mahalanobis OOD gate scored 1.294x separation but only 1.105x specificity to the actually-wrong frames — it detected *"this is Obtain"*, not *"this score is wrong"*, exactly the domain-separation artifact this paper isolates. Also independently supports the 2026-07-28 decision to attack the training objective rather than keep building on the frozen checkpoint. Read before ever trusting another cross-domain separation gate in this project. |
| PhyLatent: Learning Dynamics-Relevant Representations for JEPA World Models | Zeng, Ren, Song | [arXiv:2608.05720](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05720) (2026) | *(New, 2026-08-06.)* "Preventing global latent collapse does not ensure that a representation preserves physical states and action consequences." Names **three collapse modes distinct from global collapse** — physical invariance, physical identifiability, counterfactual dynamics — and adds three training pathways for them (physical state grounding, future representation alignment, static visual invariance, counterfactual branch separation, latent denoising). OGBench-Cube failure rates 15.60/6.71/8.41% → 7.53/0.95/4.62%; MPC success 70.0% → 78.1%, and 81.0% → 98.0% on TwoRooms with the *same architecture and planner*. **Directly explains attempt #19 Run B** (`docs/10`): our `batch_var` stayed perfectly healthy at 1.36 while effective rank crashed 26.69 → 4.50. Our new effective-rank gate detected a collapse `batch_var` couldn't see; this paper supplies the training terms that prevent the broader family of them. |
| Is Forward Prediction Enough? Physical State Grounding for JEPA World Models (PSG-JEPA) | Yan, Zhu, Jia, Yin, He, Zhong, Li, Lu, Li, Zhang, Chen, Song, Chen, Gao, Li | [arXiv:2608.06799](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06799) (2026) | *(New, 2026-08-07.)* Argues action-conditioned JEPA forward-prediction objectives "do not explicitly enforce reliable identifiability of robot-centric physical state from individual latents or state changes from latent pairs." Adds two grounding terms — individual latents → proprioceptive state, latent *pairs* → multi-horizon joint-angle changes — **applied only during training, inference architecture and cost unchanged**. Evaluated at three levels (probing, goal-conditioned planning on frozen latents, real-robot policy). This is the generalized form of our own WM v4 `InventoryDynamics` design (inventory-as-state), and the paired-latent term is the part we do *not* have: we ground the state, never the *transition*. Same family as PhyLatent above; cite both together. |
| JEPADepth: Masked Predictive Representation Learning for Self-Supervised Monocular Depth Estimation | Grigore, Popa | [arXiv:2607.26600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26600) (2026) | *(New, 2026-07-29.)* Adds a masked prediction loss in the representation space of a pretrained ViT on top of photometric depth training; best-in-class zero-shot transfer KITTI → Make3D/Cityscapes, and the predictor + target encoder are discarded at inference so deployment cost is unchanged. Monitor only for us: attempt #18 Diagnostic 1 established that off-the-shelf MiDaS depth is genuinely brightness-independent on our frames (Gate B, r=0.045, replicated) but does **not** separate tree-close from open scenes at a trustworthy sample size (Gate A failed at n=10/17). This paper is about *estimating* depth better, not about depth as a planning score — it does not address our Gate A problem. |

| ActSWM: Action-Sensitive World Models for Long-Horizon Planning in Open-World Games | Gan, Zeng, Cheng, Song, Tang, Wang | [arXiv:2607.26712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26712) (2026) | *(New, 2026-07-29.)* **The most directly actionable paper of the 2026-08-10 pass — its baseline is LeWM, i.e. our own architecture family, on Minecraft.** Names **Context Collapse**: an autoregressive latent predictor keeps high cosine similarity to true future states while producing *nearly indistinguishable futures under different action sequences* — a good `ratio` with a blind planner, which is this project's exact Phase 4/5 symptom. Measured by rolling out under recorded vs. all-zero actions and tracking the action gap Δ_k = s^gt_k − s^0_k (Eq. 10): high s^gt with small Δ = collapse. Fix = a *transition-separation principle* enforced as a rollout constraint, not an auxiliary target: a **randomly-initialized, frozen** action readout q_φ₀ whose loss backprops only into encoder/predictor (Eq. 8), plus a hinge term keeping recorded-action and zero-action rollouts apart (Eq. 5, margin m=0.3). Full objective L = L_pred + 0.09·L_sig + 0.5·L_hinge + 1.0·L_readout (Table 3, verified against the LaTeXML source, not a summary); ViT-tiny/192-dim encoder, 6-layer predictor with AdaLN action conditioning, H=32, K=12, **no EMA target**. Both new terms are train-only; planning is unchanged (CEM, horizon 12). MineStudio closed-loop Minecraft vs. LeWM at matched backbone/planner/action library, 20 trials/task (Table 8): mining 10/20 → 19/20, pillar 11/20 → 17/20, torch 19/20 → 20/20. ⚠️ The paper quotes these gains in **two different conventions** — the introduction says "up to 45% on stone mining and 30% on pillar building" (percentage points), the results section says "90.0%" and "54.5%" (relative). Both describe the same Table 8 counts; cite carefully. **Three consequences for us** (`docs/10`): (a) it targets action-sensitivity, the same axis as attempt #18 Diagnostic 2's confirmed action-coverage gap (JSD 0.1453 vs. a 0.0014 null), but via a training constraint rather than Run A's dataset broadening — which failed; (b) its anti-collapse burden is carried by the **hinge**, with SigReg only a low-weight auxiliary at λ=0.09 and **no covariance term at all** — a third configuration, distinct from both our VICReg baseline and attempt #19 Run B's SIGReg-as-full-replacement at coeff 1.0 (which hit dimensional collapse in one epoch). ⚠️ Note the paper attributes `L_sig` to **LeWM's** SigReg (Maes et al. 2026), not LeJEPA's; same lab, plausibly the same objective, but **not verified identical** — check before treating this as validation of any SIGReg configuration. (c) Their action readout is an **inverse-dynamics model** (config key `idm.stop_grad=true`, which excludes only φ₀ from the optimizer while still backpropagating through the latent inputs). Our vendored `mine_jepa/eb_jepa/losses.py::InverseDynamicsLoss` (state_t, state_t+1 → action) is structurally the `ℓ^enc` half of their Eq. 8, already wired into `VC_IDM_Sim_Regularizer` — but `build_ac_jepa` passes `idm_coeff=0.0, idm=None`, so it has never been instantiated in this project. Missing vs. ActSWM: parameter freezing, application to rollout-predicted transitions (Eq. 8b), and the hinge term entirely. ⚠️ Architecture caveat: ActSWM's predictor carries H=32 context, ours is `ACConvPredictor` with `context_length=1` — their causal story ("long context lets the predictor extrapolate scene progression while ignoring the action") cannot apply unchanged to a single-frame context. |
| JEDI: Joint Embedding Diffusion World Model for Online Model-Based Reinforcement Learning | Lim, Shah, Ikram, Yu, Ma, Leong, Liu | [arXiv:2605.13013](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13013) (2026) | *(Added 2026-08-10 — published 2026-05-13, missed by the 2026-07-27 arXiv-only pass, found via Google Scholar.)* First **online** end-to-end latent diffusion world model: learns its latent space directly from the diffusion denoising loss inside a JEPA framework, instead of reconstruction or a pretrained encoder. Theoretical contribution most relevant to us: **conventional JEPA objectives induce a predictive information bottleneck**, and conditional diffusion denoising admits a closely related predictive-compression decomposition. Competitive on Atari100k with 43% less VRAM, >3× faster WM sampling, 2.5× faster training than pixel diffusion. Bears on cold-start attempt #1's conclusion (`docs/09`) that offline-frozen-ensemble curiosity is a dead end and Plan2Explore needs its **online** training condition — this is a concrete recent instance of an online JEPA-family world model that is cheap enough to matter on consumer hardware. Not a drop-in (diffusion predictor ≠ our ACConvPredictor), so: high-value read, not a proposed swap. |
| Music-JEPA: Learning a World Model of Sound from Action | Wang, Fang, LeCun | [arXiv:2607.22000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22000) (2026) | *(New, 2026-07-24.)* LeCun's own newest action-conditioned JEPA world model: audio is the state, the pianoroll is the action, offline training, planning-via-search for transcription. Same "predict next latent state from current state + action" pattern as our eb_jepa/WM v4, validated in a new domain (music, not vision) — no technique here transfers directly to our visual/inventory problem, but it confirms the pattern's continued validity from its own originating lab. Monitor only. |

## 4. Minecraft-specific context (June 2026, refreshed 2026-08-10)

| Paper | Authors | ID / link | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| MineExplorer: Evaluating Open-World Exploration of MLLM Agents in Minecraft | Ju, Sun, Wu, Zhang, Huo, Su, Gu, Cai, Liu, Zhang | [arXiv:2605.30931](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30931) (2026) | *(Added 2026-08-10 — published 2026-05-29, missed by the 2026-07-27 arXiv-only pass, found via Google Scholar.)* Benchmark for open-world *exploration* in Minecraft, deliberately filtering out tasks that need specialist Minecraft knowledge, with rule-based milestone evaluators. Headline finding, which is our cold-start wall stated for a completely different agent class: "open-world exploration remains challenging, as strong models can handle many single-hop tasks but degrade sharply when hidden prerequisites must be coordinated over longer trajectories" — and larger models or thinking modes do **not** consistently help. Useful as external evidence that cold-start exploration in Minecraft is a hard open problem in its own right, not a defect specific to our JEPA planner. Agent class is MLLM, not a latent world model, so no technique transfers directly. |
| Solaris: Building a Multiplayer Video World Model in Minecraft | Savva, Michel, Lu, Waiwitlikhit, Meehan, Mishra, Poddar, Lu, Xie | [arXiv:2602.22208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22208) (2026) | **Multi-agent Minecraft world model** (12.64 M multiplayer frames). Generative video (not JEPA), but the most recent multi-agent-Minecraft reference — relevant to our parallel/multi-agent goal. |
| Learning To Explore With Predictive World Model Via Self-Supervised Learning | Santana, Costa, Colombini | [arXiv:2502.13200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13200) (2025) | Intrinsically-motivated agent that builds an internal world model, no external reward (Atari). Related exploration design point. |
